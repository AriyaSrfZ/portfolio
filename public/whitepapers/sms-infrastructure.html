01 // Macro Topology

The Domestic Aggregation Model & Regulatory Gatekeepers

The telecommunications landscape in Iran operates under a rigid, state-supervised aggregation hierarchy. Unlike western ecosystems where Communication Platforms as a Service (CPaaS) vendors like Twilio or Sinch establish dynamic, multi-hop global SS7/SIGTRAN signaling links with dynamic global carrier fallbacks, domestic enterprise traffic is partitioned across state-licensed wholesale aggregators and direct Mobile Network Operators (MNOs).

The primary underlying carriers—MCI (Hamrah-e Aval / Mobile Telecommunication Company of Iran), MTN Irancell, and Rightel—do not typically expose public REST endpoints to private commercial enterprises. Instead, high-volume routing is arbitrated through licensed First-Tier Aggregators via shortcode prefix allocations, enforced under regulatory oversight by the Communications Regulatory Authority (CRA) and the Telecommunication Infrastructure Company (TIC / ارتباطات زیرساخت).

CRITICAL ARCHITECTURAL REALITY: Due to structural sanctions and national cyber governance mandates, international SS7 signaling routes cannot peer directly with Iranian MNO Short Message Service Centers (SMSCs). All inbound domestic messaging must originate within white-listed domestic IP ranges through direct SMPP bindings to authorized aggregators.

03 // Low-Level Protocol Engineering

SMPP 3.4 Mechanics: PDU Lifecycles & Sliding Window Bounds

While international systems abstract transmission behind HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 REST APIs, high-throughput enterprise gateways interfacing with domestic aggregators must maintain persistent, stateful Short Message Peer-to-Peer (SMPP) v3.4 socket sessions over raw TCP.

Binding Session Topology

Gateways establish separate asynchronous TCP connections: bind_transmitter (TX) for outbound injection, bind_receiver (RX) for incoming delivery receipts and inbound replies, or bind_transceiver (TRX) for bidirectional multiplexing. MNOs enforce maximum binding limits (often max 2-5 concurrent TCP binds per enterprise account).

Sliding Window & Backpressure

Synchronous dispatch (awaiting submit_sm_resp before emitting the next PDU) caps throughput at ~10 TPS due to network round-trip time. Gateways implement a Sliding Window protocol: emitting up to $W$ in-flight unacknowledged PDUs. If window size $W$ exceeds carrier thresholds (typically 16-64), the SMSC throttles with ESME_RTHROTTLED (0x00000058).

Heartbeat & Session Health

To keep TCP socket state alive through stateful carrier NAT firewalls, the client must emit enquire_link PDUs at strict intervals (typically 30 seconds). Failure to receive an enquire_link_resp within the timeout window demands aggressive TCP teardown, socket draining, and reconnection to a fallback aggregator node.

04 // Regulatory Impact Analysis

The Systemic Impact of RCS & MMS Protocol Blocking

1. Regulatory Context & Attack Surface Mitigation

In global telecommunications, Rich Communication Services (RCS) via Universal Profile 2.0+ enables carrier-verified sender branding, high-resolution media payloads, interactive carousel buttons, and end-to-end encryption via Google Jibe or carrier clouds.

In Iran, RCS is structurally unavailable. Google Jibe servers and carrier-level data signaling routes for RCS are disabled or unreachable due to national cyberspace filtering policies and the National Information Network (NIN / شبکه ملی اطلاعات) architecture. Similarly, MMS (Multimedia Messaging Service) has remained deactivated or heavily throttled for mass enterprise distribution since early regulatory bans over multimedia content inspection concerns.

2. Engineering Fallout: The GSM-7 & UCS-2 Trap

Because rich data channels are blocked, 100% of all critical transactional and conversational touchpoints—including banking OTPs, debit/credit receipts, logistics tracking, marketing campaigns, and two-factor authentication—are forced backward onto legacy 1990s-era GSM signaling channels.

This creates immense technical and financial penalties:

  • Persian UCS-2 Encoding Overhead: Persian text cannot be encoded using standard 7-bit GSM default alphabet (160 characters). It requires 16-bit UCS-2 encoding, restricting single SMS length to just 70 characters.
  • UDH Concatenation Tax: Messages exceeding 70 characters inject a 6-byte User Data Header (UDH), reducing per-segment capacity to 67 characters. A complex bank receipt or e-commerce delivery notification easily splits into 3 to 5 concatenated parts, multiplying billing cost and SMSC congestion by 300% to 500%.
  • Missing Delivery of Split Segments: In concatenated messages, if segment 2 of 3 is dropped during BSC radio handovers, the subscriber handset cannot reconstruct the payload, rendering banking alerts completely unreadable.

05 // Delivery Telemetry

DLR Lifecycle Reconciliation & Cell-Tower LBS Filtering

Delivery Receipt (DLR) State Machine

When an enterprise submits a submit_sm PDU, the aggregator returns a message_id in the synchronous submit_sm_resp. This only proves receipt by the aggregator, NOT delivery to the mobile handset.

Handset delivery confirmation travels asynchronously over hours via an incoming deliver_sm PDU containing the receipt text payload:

id:10982348 sub:001 dlvrd:001 submit date:2609151230 done date:2609151231 stat:DELIVRD err:000

Gateways must match id with internal billing records. Primary status codes: DELIVRD (Reached handset), UNDELIV (Out of coverage / unreachable), EXPIRED (SMSC timeout reached, typically 48 hours), or REJECTD (DND blacklisted).

Location-Based Services (LBS) via Cell-ID

Domestic operators offer specialized LBS (Location-Based SMS) engines. Instead of relying on GPS or client-side app permissions, targeting operates directly on cellular network signaling data via the Home Location Register (HLR) and Visitor Location Register (VLR).

Enterprises define geographic polygons or target specific Base Transceiver Stations (BTS / cell towers). When a subscriber's mobile phone registers a periodic location update or handovers between cells (e.g., entering an exhibition center, airport, or specific trade district), the operator's LBS switch triggers an automated transactional or marketing SMS.

SLA LATENCY CONSTRAINT: LBS injection suffers from 5 to 45 minute propagation delays due to VLR batch updates and carrier queue limits.

07 // Architecture Trade-offs

Latency, Resilience & Rate-Limiting Dilemmas

Aggregator Failover vs. Message Deduplication

When an upstream aggregator (e.g., Magfa 3000) experiences an outage or TCP socket stall during a national traffic spike, an enterprise system must decide whether to reroute pending OTPs to a secondary aggregator (e.g., 1000 or 9000). However, if the first aggregator unblocks later and emits the queued message, the subscriber receives duplicate OTPs, invalidating the first session hash. Systems must trade off failover speed against client confusion.

Synchronous Ingestion vs. Backpressure Buffering

Enterprise microservices generate bursts of 10,000+ requests per second during promotional flashes or authentication surges. Domestic aggregator bindings cap ingestion at 100-300 TPS. Gateways must implement persistent distributed queues (Kafka or RabbitMQ) with leaky-bucket rate shapers. If backpressure retention exceeds 5 minutes, delayed OTPs arrive after the client-side UI timer expires, destroying conversion.
